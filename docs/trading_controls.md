# Trading Controls in Energy Risk Management

Trading controls are systems and processes that govern, monitor, and limit trading activity to manage risk within defined tolerance levels.

## Why Trading Controls Matter
Energy trading involves large notional values, complex instruments, and volatile markets. Without robust controls, a single trader or system error can cause catastrophic losses.

## Types of Trading Controls

### Pre-Trade Controls
Limits and checks applied before an order is executed:
- Position limits: Maximum allowable exposure per commodity, tenor, and trader
- Order size limits: Maximum single order size
- Price reasonability checks: Reject orders at prices far from market
- Counterparty credit limits: Cap exposure to individual counterparties

### Post-Trade Controls
Monitoring applied after execution:
- P&L attribution: Explain daily P&L by risk factor
- Limit breach alerts: Real-time notification when limits are approached or breached
- Trade reconciliation: Match front office trades with back office records
- Valuation controls: Independent price verification against market data

### Market Risk Controls
- VaR limits: Portfolio VaR must stay within approved thresholds
- Stress test limits: Portfolio must survive defined stress scenarios
- Greeks limits: Delta, gamma, vega limits for options books

## Governance Framework
Trading controls operate within a three-lines-of-defence model:
1. First line: Trading desk self-monitoring and desk risk managers
2. Second line: Independent risk management function
3. Third line: Internal audit

## Technology and Automation
Modern trading controls rely on real-time risk systems:
- ETRM systems (e.g. Endur, Allegro) for trade capture and position keeping
- Risk engines for real-time VaR and Greeks calculation
- Pre-trade limit checking integrated into order management systems
- Automated alerts and escalation workflows

## Regulatory Requirements
Energy trading firms must comply with:
- REMIT (EU): Reporting and market abuse rules for wholesale energy
- EMIR: Derivatives reporting and clearing obligations
- MiFID II: Best execution and transaction reporting
- Dodd-Frank (US): Similar derivatives reporting requirements
